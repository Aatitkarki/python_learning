## Before the exercises: Graphs, checkpoints, and human review

### Concepts in plain language

A state graph describes operations as nodes and transitions as edges. START and END delimit execution. A checkpoint stores enough state to continue later. An in-memory checkpointer loses state when its process exits; durable storage survives restart.

An interrupt pauses before a sensitive action. Approval must match the exact stored proposal and a verified identity. A fingerprint detects changed content, but does not authenticate the person presenting it. Thread/run IDs separate conversations and cannot replace authorization.

A two-worker design may split research and checking, but extra workers introduce coordination and extra calls. Compare equal tasks under equal budgets, tracking correctness, latency, token use, and failure rates.
