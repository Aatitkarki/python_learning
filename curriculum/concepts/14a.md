## Before the exercises: Reading financial quantities and ratios

Read this in small pieces. Explain each new term in your own words, trace the example on paper, and change one input before continuing. If the arithmetic is unfamiliar, use [the math bridge](../MATH_START_HERE.md).

### Concepts in plain language

Use these fictional statements to learn arithmetic, not investment recommendations. Revenue is sales for a period; expenses reduce income. Assets are resources, liabilities are obligations, and equity is the residual: assets=liabilities+equity. Cash flow and accounting income differ.

A ratio divides compatible quantities and needs a valid denominator. Operating margin is operating income/revenue. Free cash flow in this course is operating cash flow minus capital expenditures. EPS is net income divided by weighted-average shares. If both amounts and shares are in millions, the millions cancel and EPS is currency per share.

Growth compares a current figure to a comparable prior period: `(current-prior)/prior`. P/E divides share price by positive EPS. ROE/ROA use average opening/closing equity/assets. Missing history, incompatible units, or undefined denominators must remain explicit.

### Worked example: follow the values

Net income 12 million and shares 3 million gives EPS 4 dollars/share. A price of 40 dollars gives P/E 10. Revenue growing from 80 to 100 has growth .25, or 25%. A PEG convention using percentage points divides P/E by 25, not .25.
