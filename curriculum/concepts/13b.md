## Before the exercises: Comparisons, uncertainty, and release gates

Read this in small pieces. Explain each new term in your own words, trace the example on paper, and change one input before continuing. If the arithmetic is unfamiliar, use [the math bridge](../MATH_START_HERE.md).

### Concepts in plain language

Compare a baseline and candidate on the same cases. A paired difference subtracts baseline score from candidate score per case. Averages describe the observed set; uncertainty procedures examine how that estimate varies under assumptions.

A bootstrap samples cases with replacement and recomputes the difference. When cases share a document, resample whole document groups to retain dependence. A confidence interval spanning zero means the direction is uncertain under the procedure, not proof that the methods are identical.

A release gate is a condition declared before evaluation. Some failures, such as a permission leak or wrong critical number, should block release independently of average quality. A CLI exits nonzero on a failed gate so automation can detect it.

### Worked example: follow the values

Baseline scores `[0,1,1]` and candidate `[1,1,1]` give differences `[1,0,0]`, mean improvement 1/3. If the first two cases share a source, resample their group together. Always include counts for each category, since a perfect score on one case is weak evidence.
